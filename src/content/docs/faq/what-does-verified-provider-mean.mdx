A verified provider in Agent Registrar holds a signed provider verification credential from a trusted broker binding its canonical origin, discovery audience, and assurance level. At `domain` level, a broker checked HTTPS or DNS control of that origin; at `entity` level, an operator also reviewed legal-entity registration and official contact evidence.

## How it works

Under Agent Identity Protocol `v2.3.0`, providers publish an optional `attestation` source (`broker`-managed or `provider`-managed) in `/.well-known/agent-signup.json`. When an agent calls `identity_signup` or `identity_login` via the five-tool MCP server (`identity_whoami`, `identity_signup`, `identity_login`, `identity_record_signup`, `inbox_read`), the broker evaluates that credential against the human owner's `/trust` policy:

- `verify` (default): requires a valid `broker`-managed or `provider`-managed verification credential.
- `strict`: requires a valid `broker`-managed credential only.
- `no_verify`: allows unverified providers while recording their unverified status in `/activity`.

You can compare verified, unverified, and cross-broker providers live on the [Demo Network](https://agentregistrardemobroker.com).

## Limits

Verification proves origin or legal-entity control at `checked_at` - it never means safe, approved, endorsed, audited, or reliable. Verified providers still receive contact details in the assertion and can misbehave; humans retain per-provider blocks, per-agent kill switches, and `/trust/report`. See [Trust and limits](/start/trust-and-limits/).

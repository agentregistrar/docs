Service providers curb free-tier abuse by requiring brokered AI agent signups backed by a verified human owner rather than anonymous scripts. Under Agent Identity Protocol v2.3.0, each signed assertion carries a stable human identifier (`human.id`), verified email status (`human.verified`), and hash-pinned terms acceptance so providers can enforce per-human quotas.

## How it works

Instead of letting automated tools spin up unlimited disposable email accounts, key free-tier accounts on `(iss, human.id)` rather than `agent.email` alone. When a developer signs up via Agent Registrar's five-tool MCP (`identity_whoami`, `identity_signup`, `identity_login`, `identity_record_signup`, `inbox_read`), all agents owned by that human share the same `human.id` across `endpoints.agent_signup` and Day-2 `endpoints.agent_login`. You can also require a verified domain credential (`attestation`) and try free-tier flows in the isolated Demo Network at [agentregistrardemobroker.com](https://agentregistrardemobroker.com) and [reference.agentregistrardemorp.com](https://reference.agentregistrardemorp.com).

## Limits

Agent Registrar does not enforce your quotas, block free-tier signups for you, or perform government ID checks (`human.verified` means email OTP verification). Providers still own rate limits, billing gates, and fraud rules; see [Trust and limits](/start/trust-and-limits/).

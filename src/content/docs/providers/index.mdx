A self-serve signup flow usually assumes a human at a browser: a form, an email inbox and a click. Brokered signup gives a provider a verified human owner and a hash-pinned record of which terms the agent accepted. If the provider issues a receipt, it also gets a signed attribution proof. The provider keeps its own account model, credentials and product API.

The Agent Identity Protocol (`agent-identity-protocol` on the wire) is an
identity and brokerage protocol. It does not sell, provision or describe your
product. This section is the implementation path: publish one document, verify
one signed assertion, return a stable answer.

## The three-step path

1. **Publish discovery.** Serve `GET /.well-known/agent-signup.json` with the identity fields and `terms: { version, url }` pointing at your hosted agreement. Start with [Publish discovery](/providers/guides/publish-discovery/).
2. **Verify assertions.** Accept `POST` at `endpoints.agent_signup`, verify the Ed25519 JWT against the issuer's JWKS, and enforce the checks. Start with [Verify assertions](/providers/guides/verify-assertions/).
3. **Validate and register.** Confirm the document with the conformance validator, then submit a registry entry with the evidence. Start with [Validate and register](/providers/guides/validate-and-register/).

The [provider quickstart](/providers/quickstart/) walks discovery, terms and
validation in one pass, then links to verification and registration.

If you use a provider account, the portal reduces setup to four steps: create the
service (display name, origin, audience), verify the domain, copy the generated
discovery document, and run one synchronous setup test that marks the service
live. The optional provider verification credential (an `attestation` object in
discovery) is broker-managed by default, so no provider signing key is needed to
accept signups.

## What you publish, check and return

| Artifact | Where | What it proves |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Discovery document | `GET /.well-known/agent-signup.json` | Which issuers you accept, what scopes you need, the provider-hosted terms URL and version, and the one signup endpoint. |
| Assertion | `Authorization: Bearer <jwt>` at signup | The agent, its verified human owner, the delegated scopes and the accepted terms URL and hash. |
| Signup receipt and context pointer | Signup response, optional | Identity facts for attribution, an opaque account id and a hash-pinned link to your guide. |

The field-level rules are in [Discovery fields](/protocol/discovery-fields/), [Assertion claims](/protocol/assertion-claims/) and [Receipt and context](/protocol/receipt-and-context/). The normative contract is the [specification](/protocol/specification/).

## What you do not need

- **No broker account to publish or verify.** The broker mints assertions and does not call your signup endpoint. The JWKS is public. The optional receipt is recorded after signup, not during the handshake.
- **No provider signing key to accept signups.** Broker-managed attestations accept `keys: []`. A key is optional and is used only for provider-signed receipts or authenticated abuse reports.
- **No catalog in discovery.** Product capabilities stay in your own post-signup guide.
- **No SDK dependency.** The protocol is one JSON document and one HTTP endpoint. Examples use `jose` or Web Crypto; any stack works.

## What you own

- The terms document at a stable `terms.url`, its version, and re-acceptance when the agreement changes.
- The account model, idempotent resolution and show-once credentials.
- The stable error envelope and your rate limits.
- The optional receipt and the context guide, including hash updates.
- Your abuse and security contact, and reports to the broker.

<Aside type="note" title="What conformance means">
A valid discovery document is a statement about the document, not about your security, uptime or product. Discovery conformance is cheap to publish. The protocol does not prevent abuse; it makes accountability and revocation possible.
</Aside>
